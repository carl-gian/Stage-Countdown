Stage Countdown, in the Steward look

A bright countdown on frosted glass, with a gently moving nature scene behind it. Three scenes to choose from, each with a light (day) and a dark (night) look, and two kinds of motion: Calm or Plain. The stage below really runs: it plays the start, counts down, and shows the last seconds and the end.

1 · Pick a scene

Simple shapes and a few slow movements: clouds drift, a bird or two glides past, trees sway a little, light shimmers. Click one to play it on the big screen below.

2 · The stage screen

The numbers sit on Apple-style frosted glass: a heavy blur, a bright rim and light catching the top edge. The scene behind is softened so it stays quiet, and shows through the glass blurred. Digits slide in as they change, the colon ticks each second, the ring shrinks as time runs out, and the last 10 seconds turn amber. Motion: Calm drifts slowly, Plain stands still.

5 · What would change, and what wouldn't

  • The sequences stay exactly as they are.Hiding M³, the black screen, the 4.5 s before counting, the 10 s hold at 00:00 and the hand-back keep their timings. The mockup's “Play the start” uses the same 1 s black, 1 s reveal and 2 s hold.
  • The countdown gets its own rules.Steward keeps stage screens dark and their art still, because those rules were written for talk timers. A countdown plays before the meeting, while no one is speaking, so I'd write it down in the design repo as its own case: a bright day look, a slowly moving scene (or Plain), and the numbers always on frosted glass.
  • Light or dark is your choice, or “Auto”.Auto could follow Windows, or the time of day: day look for morning meetings, night look for evening ones.
  • The ring shrinks instead of filling up.It shows the time that is left. Easy to flip back if you prefer the old way.
  • Reduced motion is respected.When Windows asks for less motion the stage behaves as Plain, the digits change without moving, and the end ripples don't play.
  • Your saved colours are kept.Anyone with custom colours keeps them under “Advanced”; everyone else gets the new look.
